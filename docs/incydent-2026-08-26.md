# Incident, 26 August 2026 — the chatbot went silent and nobody was told

**Severity:** the product's primary function was unavailable for one tenant for
roughly a day.
**Detected:** by accident, while verifying an unrelated change.
**Tenant affected:** our own — the agency site, `agencjasm-art.pl`.

---

## Summary

A 14-day trial subscription ended on 25 August. From midnight on 26 August the
chat widget stopped answering. Every visitor who opened it and asked a question
received **"Wystąpił błąd. Spróbuj ponownie."** — an error message asking them to
retry something that could not succeed.

No alert was raised. No log line was written. Nothing in the panel changed
appearance. The outage was found the next day, by chance, while working through
a manual verification checklist for a different change.

The tenant was our own, so no customer was harmed. The same code path applies to
every customer, which is the reason this is written down.

---

## Impact

| | |
|---|---|
| **Window** | 26 Aug 00:00 (server time) → 26 Aug, when the subscription was extended |
| **Affected** | Every visitor to `agencjasm-art.pl` who opened the chat and sent a message |
| **Behaviour** | Chat accepted the message, then replied with a generic error |
| **Data loss** | None |
| **Money** | None directly. Any sales enquiry that would have come through the chat in that window was lost silently. |

**How many visitors were affected is unknown, and that is part of the finding.**
The refusal happens in `SubscriptionMiddleware`, before the request reaches any
view — so no conversation, no message and no event was recorded. The outage left
no trace in our own data. We cannot count what we did not log.

## Detection

The honest version: **we did not detect this. We tripped over it.**

The checklist step that found it existed to verify something else entirely — that
the widget still worked after a change to how panel sessions are stored. The
widget authenticates with an API key and has nothing to do with sessions, so it
was on the list only as a precaution.

Had that step not been on the list, the trial would have stayed expired until
somebody happened to open the chat on the agency's own website.

**Time from failure to detection: ~1 day. Time to fix once detected: minutes.**
The gap was entirely in noticing.

---

## Root cause

`SubscriptionMiddleware` requires `start_date <= today <= end_date` and refuses
otherwise. That check is correct and should stay. The failure was not in the
check — it was that **nothing told anyone the check had started refusing**.

The system already had alerts for the *other* way a chatbot goes silent:
`accounts/tasks.py` sends the owner an email at 80%, 95% and 100% of the message
limit. Its docstring states the intent plainly:

> Wyczerpanie limitu wygląda z zewnątrz jak awaria chatbota (…) Alerty mają
> zamienić cichą awarię w decyzję.

The same reasoning applies word for word to a subscription running out of days.
It had simply never been extended to that case. A date passing is less vivid than
a counter filling up, and it was missed.

---

## Contributing factors

**1. The visitor was told to retry.** "Spróbuj ponownie" is correct advice for a
network blip and actively misleading for a permanent condition. The visitor
retried, failed again, and closed the tab — with no other route offered, even
though the widget already had a contact form for exactly this kind of dead end.

**2. A second, unrelated 403 was hiding underneath.** The network trace showed
`/api/widget-settings/` also refused, with *"Twój plan obejmuje 1 witrynę, a
widget działa już na tylu."* The site registry records the `Origin` header, and
the chat window runs inside an iframe served from our own panel — so
`panel.agencjasm-art.pl` was being registered as one of the customer's websites
and consuming their plan's only domain slot. This affected every customer on a
one-domain plan, not just us. It would not have been found without the trace
taken for the first problem.

**3. The refusal path is silent by design.** `SubscriptionMiddleware` returns a
`JsonResponse` directly. It writes no log line and records no event. There is
nothing to alert on, nothing to graph and nothing to count after the fact.

---

## What we changed

**Owner is told, twice.** A daily scheduled task warns three days before the
subscription ends and reports again once it has. The message says explicitly that
visitors are seeing the failure — not only that something changed in the panel,
because that distinction is what turns a notice into a decision. The
"already notified" marker is tied to the end date rather than the threshold, so a
renewal resets it and the warnings work again on the next cycle.
(`accounts/tasks_konce.py`, 10 tests.)

**Visitor is told the truth and offered a way out.** All refusals that mean "this
chat will not answer, and retrying will not help" now carry
`kod: czat_niedostepny`. The widget recognises it, stops asking for a retry, and
offers the contact form instead — the same mechanism it already uses when the bot
has no grounded answer. A failed conversation becomes an enquiry instead of a
closed tab. The message deliberately does not mention subscriptions or limits: a
business's billing is not its visitors' concern. (5 backend tests, 5 frontend
tests.)

**Our own domain no longer consumes a customer's slot.** The site registry
excludes the host from `FRONTEND_URL`, and migration `0023` removes the entries
that had already been recorded — for every customer, not just the one who found
it. (3 tests.)

---

## What we deliberately did not change

**The subscription check itself.** Refusing service after a subscription expires
is correct. The bug was the silence around it, not the refusal.

**Telling the visitor why.** It is tempting to show "this business's subscription
has expired" — it would be honest and would put pressure on the right person. It
would also air a customer's billing status to their customers. The owner learns
the reason by email; the visitor learns only that the chat is unavailable.

---

## Verification

Every fix was checked by breaking it on purpose and confirming a test caught it —
removing the notification's date binding, making the widget treat every error as
unavailability, and widening the domain exclusion to any host. Each landed on the
test written for it.

The end state was measured against production from a real browser, not asserted:

```
200  /api/widget-settings/?lang=pl   (from the client page)
200  /api/widget-settings/           (from inside the iframe — was 403)
200  /api/widget/chat/stream/        (was 403; streams a grounded answer)
```

---

## What this leaves open

- **The refusal path still writes no log line.** The alerts mean an expiry will
  now be noticed, but a refusal for any other reason remains invisible and
  uncountable. Logging middleware refusals is not done.
- **No monitoring exists.** There is no dashboard on which a chatbot going quiet
  would be visible, and no alert on a drop in conversation volume — which is the
  signal that would have caught this in minutes rather than a day.
- **The check that found this was manual.** A step on a checklist a person walks
  through is not detection. It happened to be there.
